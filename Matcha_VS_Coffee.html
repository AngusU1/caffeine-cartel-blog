Matcha vs Coffee: Why More People Are Switching to Matcha

An investigation into the green powder controlling the streets

Coffee has long been the go-to caffeine source—but matcha is rapidly gaining ground. From wellness circles to office desks, matcha is becoming the drink of choice for people looking for a calmer kind of energy.

What Is Matcha?

Matcha is a finely ground powder made from specially grown green tea leaves. Unlike regular tea, where leaves are steeped and removed, you consume the entire leaf, making matcha more concentrated in nutrients and caffeine.

Why Are People Switching from Coffee to Matcha?

Coffee delivers a sharp caffeine spike followed by a crash. Matcha contains L-theanine, an amino acid that slows caffeine absorption, providing:

1. Smother Energy Fewer Crashes

  • Calm, sustained Energy
  • Less Jitters
  • Improved Focus

2. Better for Anxiety and Stress

Many people find coffee worsens anxiety. Matcha’s combination of caffeine and L-theanine promotes alertness without overstimulation.

3. Easier on the stomach

Matcha is less acidic than coffee, making it gentler on digestion and teeth.

4. Health & Antioxidant Appeal

Matcha is packed with antioxidants, particularly EGCG, often linked to heart health and metabolism.

Coffee vs Matcha: The Final Verdict

  • Coffee is stronger and faster
  • Matcha is steadier and calmer
  • Coffee is habitual, matcha is intentional

For many, matcha isn’t a replacement—it’s a complement. Coffee for mornings, matcha for afternoons.
